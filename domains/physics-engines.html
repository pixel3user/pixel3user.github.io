Physics Engines

Robotics physics and particle simulation

Robot control where contacts are discovered by the optimiser instead of scripted in advance. The formulation treats generalised positions as decision variables, models compliant contact with regularised Coulomb friction, and solves the resulting sparse nonlinear least-squares problem fast enough for closed-loop hardware control.

Tools surveyed in this file: MuJoCo · Geant4

How this page is organised: engineering decision first, visual mechanism second, claim-by-claim audit third, and the retained files that would settle the claim last.

The engineering decision

Can a contact-implicit optimiser discover make/break contact fast enough to close the loop on quadrupeds, dexterous hands and a 20-DOF bi-manual system?

This question — not the software logo list — is the organising test for the case. Every visual and artefact below either helps answer it or exposes what is still only an assertion in the survey text.

Failure modes worth probing

  • Regularised friction permits slow object drift that looks stable in short clips but fails longer manipulation.
  • Warm starts hide solver fragility until a contact change makes the previous solution a poor basin.
  • Diagonal scaling improves one morphology and destabilises another with very different inertia ratios.
  • Average loop rate passes while occasional factorisation spikes miss the control deadline.

Three questions that reveal depth

  1. What residuals and equality constraints encode inverse dynamics and contact?
  2. How are infeasible or timed-out solves handled at the control boundary?
  3. Which benchmark was hardest: quadruped contact switching or in-hand manipulation, and why?

Execution sequence

Real-time contact-implicit MPC via inverse-dynamics optimisation

The source brief in delivery order, rewritten as an inspectable process. Each step should leave behind one of the artefacts in the dossier above; a step with no surviving output is where a reviewer should press hardest.

  1. Formulate contact-implicit MPC

    Transcribe the multi-body system into a sparse equality-constrained nonlinear least-squares problem, with generalised positions as the primary decision variables.

  2. Model contact honestly

    Use compliant contact with regularised Coulomb friction, so the solver can make and break contacts without a pre-specified contact schedule.

  3. Build a solver that keeps up

    Implement a trust-region Gauss–Newton optimiser with an equality-constrained dogleg step, diagonal Hessian scaling diag(D) = diag(H)^(−1/4), and sparse block-banded factorisation.

  4. Validate across morphologies

    Closed-loop tests in Drake: Mini Cheetah quadruped trotting at 60 Hz and Allegro-hand in-hand manipulation, both with dynamic contact switching.

  5. Close the loop on hardware

    Real-time control at 100–200 Hz on a 20-DOF bi-manual manipulator, with loop-rate distributions recorded as the acceptance evidence.

Expected deliverables

  • C++ CI-MPC solver (Drake + MuJoCo backends)
  • Formulation notes: residuals, constraints, scaling
  • Simulation validation suite (quadruped + dexterous hand)
  • Hardware loop-rate benchmark report

How quality is validated

  • Contact switches resolved without pre-specified schedules
  • 60 Hz closed loop in quadruped simulation
  • 100–200 Hz demonstrated on 20-DOF hardware
  • Solver timing distributions, not single best-case numbers
