Electronics Design

Schematics, PCB layout, and circuit simulation

Power electronics design where simulation comes before layout and layout is treated as a circuit in its own right. High-current switching stages and millivolt-level current sensing share one board, so copper geometry, plane strategy, and decoupling are engineered — and then proven on the bench with oscilloscope, logic analyser, and thermal camera.

Tools surveyed in this file: KiCad · LibrePCB · DesignSpark PCB · Ngspice · LTspice · Qucs-S

How this page is organised: engineering decision first, visual mechanism second, claim-by-claim audit third, and the retained files that would settle the claim last.

The engineering decision

Can an STM32F4 FOC board switch 30 A phases hard enough for control while keeping millivolt current feedback electrically quiet?

This question — not the software logo list — is the organising test for the case. Every visual and artefact below either helps answer it or exposes what is still only an assertion in the survey text.

Failure modes worth probing

  • Gate-loop inductance creates Miller turn-on even though the DC design and slow bench tests look sound.
  • Shared copper at the shunt makes current feedback depend on phase current distribution and temperature.
  • Bootstrap supply collapses at extreme duty cycle, so failures appear only near modulation limits.
  • Thermal vias help the MOSFET tab while the connector or shunt becomes the hidden hot spot.

Three questions that reveal depth

  1. Where exactly was ripple measured, and with what probe bandwidth/grounding?
  2. What protects the board during a stalled motor or phase-to-phase fault?
  3. Which components set the real continuous-current limit: MOSFET, shunt, connector, or copper?

Execution sequence

4-layer field-oriented-control (FOC) motor-driver board

The source brief in delivery order, rewritten as an inspectable process. Each step should leave behind one of the artefacts in the dossier above; a step with no surviving output is where a reviewer should press hardest.

  1. Capture a hierarchical schematic

    Structure the design in KiCad around an STM32F4 MCU, three-phase half-bridge gate drivers, and low-side current shunts, with IPC-2221 design rules enforced from the first sheet.

  2. Simulate before layout

    Model power-rail decoupling, MOSFET gate-drive transients, and bootstrap charging in LTspice — cutting regulator ripple from 120 mV to 28 mV and removing high-frequency ringing before tape-out.

  3. Lay out power and sense separately

    Route 2 oz outer copper pours with thermal via arrays for 30 A continuous phase current, sandwiching solid internal ground planes to shield differential current-sense traces from switching noise.

  4. Release a complete fab package

    Generate Gerber RS-274X, drill tables, pick-and-place centroids, and a BOM cross-referenced to distributor part numbers so procurement never blocks the build.

  5. Bring up on the bench

    Validate power sequencing and switching waveforms with a 4-channel 200 MHz oscilloscope, logic analyser, and thermal camera — first-pass success under full dynamometer motor load.

Expected deliverables

  • Hierarchical KiCad schematic (power, MCU, gate drive, I/O)
  • LTspice simulation deck: gate drive, bootstrap, decoupling
  • Gerber RS-274X + drill + pick-and-place fabrication files
  • Distributor-cross-referenced BOM
  • Bench bring-up report with scope captures

How quality is validated

  • Regulator ripple reduced 120 mV → 28 mV in simulation, confirmed on bench
  • Gate-source overshoot held under 5% on scope captures
  • 30 A continuous thermal performance checked with thermal camera
  • First-pass operation under full dynamometer load
