2D & 3D Art

Digital painting, illustration, pixel art, and 3D modeling

This domain covers the full real-time art pipeline — from greybox blocking and modular mesh authoring to texture packing, LOD and collision setup, and in-engine lighting and profiling. The emphasis is technical art discipline: grid snapping so pieces tile without seams, texel-density budgeting so surfaces read consistently, and draw-call control so a rich scene still hits frame budget.

Tools surveyed in this file: GIMP · Inkscape · Krita · LibreSprite · Blender

How this page is organised: engineering decision first, visual mechanism second, claim-by-claim audit third, and the retained files that would settle the claim last.

The engineering decision

Can thirty reusable pieces produce a rich Gothic interior without bespoke geometry for every wall — and still leave enough frame time for gameplay?

This question — not the software logo list — is the organising test for the case. Every visual and artefact below either helps answer it or exposes what is still only an assertion in the survey text.

Failure modes worth probing

  • Texel density drifts between hero props and architecture, so the kit reads as several unrelated asset packs.
  • Trim-sheet padding is too small and colour bleeds appear only after mipmapping at gameplay distance.
  • Collision hulls are copied from render meshes, making traces unpredictable around arches and trims.
  • Lumen/fog settings are tuned for one still and collapse when the camera moves through the full hall.

Three questions that reveal depth

  1. Which pieces are true modules and which are one-off hero meshes?
  2. What texel-density target was used, and where is it allowed to break?
  3. How many materials and draw calls does the assembled hall use at the worst camera position?

Execution sequence

Modular Gothic environment kit for Unreal Engine 5

The source brief in delivery order, rewritten as an inspectable process. Each step should leave behind one of the artefacts in the dossier above; a step with no surviving output is where a reviewer should press hardest.

  1. Model the modular kit

    Build a 30-piece architectural kit plus hero props in Blender on a strict 10 cm grid, with clean manifold geometry so every wall, floor, and trim piece snaps together without gaps or z-fighting.

  2. Surface with painted PBR textures

    Hand-paint stylised diffuse detail and curvature masks in Krita, then composite trim sheets in GIMP, packing Roughness, Metallic, and Ambient Occlusion into a single RMA texture to cut texture memory and draw calls.

  3. Bake, LOD, and collide

    Bake high-poly bevel detail down to optimised in-game LODs and author custom collision hulls per piece, so gameplay traces stay cheap and predictable.

  4. Assemble and light in engine

    Import the library into Unreal Engine 5 behind a master material with vertex-painted dirt and wear, populate with modular foliage, and light with real-time Lumen global illumination and volumetric fog.

  5. Profile to frame budget

    Profile the assembled scene end to end — draw calls, texture memory, Lumen cost — and tune until the environment holds a solid 60 FPS in an interactive walkthrough.

Expected deliverables

  • 30-piece modular mesh library with LODs and collision hulls
  • Trim-sheet set: Base Color, Normal, packed RMA channels
  • UE5 master material with vertex-paint wear controls
  • Assembled, lit environment level with profiling notes

How quality is validated

  • 10 cm grid conformance checked across all kit pieces
  • Texel density held consistent across repeating architecture
  • 60 FPS target verified by in-engine profiling
  • Wireframe and UV-density views reviewed piece by piece
