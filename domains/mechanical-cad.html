Mechanical CAD

Parts, assemblies, and 2D drawings

Mechanical design taken from parametric model to released manufacturing package. The work centres on fit-critical rotating machinery — gear tooth profiles, bearing pockets, and housing interfaces — where tolerance decisions, not modelling speed, determine whether the first machined article assembles without rework.

Tools surveyed in this file: FreeCAD · OpenSCAD · SolveSpace · Rhino 3D · AutoCAD Mechanical · Autodesk Inventor · LibreCAD / QCAD CE · BricsCAD

How this page is organised: engineering decision first, visual mechanism second, claim-by-claim audit third, and the retained files that would settle the claim last.

The engineering decision

Will a 42-part cycloidal actuator assemble with the intended mesh and bearing fits on the first CNC run, across machining variation and thermal growth?

This question — not the software logo list — is the organising test for the case. Every visual and artefact below either helps answer it or exposes what is still only an assertion in the survey text.

Fit logic

The stack-up is the argument, not the CAD screenshot

How to read it: Ask for the signed stack-up spreadsheet/model and the drawing revision it belongs to. A pretty assembly render cannot show press-fit or thermal-growth margin.

Ratio check

35:1 is arithmetic; torque quality is a test

How to read it: The reviewer should separate ratio (countable) from zero-backlash and efficiency claims (measurable only on a rig with encoders and a torque transducer).

Failure modes worth probing

  • Eccentricity and pin-hole position consume the same tolerance budget twice, producing periodic tight spots.
  • Housing distortion after unclamping shifts bearing bores even though in-process probing looked acceptable.
  • Thermal growth turns a correct room-temperature press fit into preload loss or excessive drag.
  • Backlash is measured unloaded and reappears under torque through pin/housing compliance.

Three questions that reveal depth

  1. Which single fit controls backlash, and which controls assembly force?
  2. What datum scheme makes the pin ring inspectable on a CMM?
  3. At what torque and temperature was the zero-rework claim established?

Execution sequence

High-reduction cycloidal robotic actuator gearbox (35:1)

The source brief in delivery order, rewritten as an inspectable process. Each step should leave behind one of the artefacts in the dossier above; a step with no surviving output is where a reviewer should press hardest.

  1. Model the 42-part assembly

    Define parametric cycloidal gear tooth profiles, eccentric stages, and bearing pocket fits in FreeCAD and Autodesk Inventor, keeping every fit dimension driven from the master model.

  2. Prove the fits on paper first

    Run 1D and 3D worst-case tolerance stack-ups to guarantee zero-backlash mesh engagement and correct needle-bearing press fits across the thermal expansion range — before any metal is cut.

  3. Release a GD&T drawing package

    Author 2D manufacturing drawings to ASME Y14.5 with position, concentricity, and runout callouts tied to clear datum references, so any competent shop can make interchangeable parts.

  4. Design with the machine shop

    Work DFM adjustments directly with CNC vendors: mill-path-friendly 6061-T6 housing geometry, standard dowel pins and seals, and no feature that needs a setup the shop does not have.

  5. Assemble, dyno-test, release the BOM

    Take the design through physical prototype assembly and dynamometer torque testing to a released bill of materials — 35:1 reduction with zero assembly rework on the first production run.

Expected deliverables

  • Parametric 42-part assembly model (STEP-exportable)
  • ASME Y14.5 GD&T 2D drawing package
  • Tolerance stack-up analysis (1D + 3D worst case)
  • Released BOM with bearings, pins, seals, and stock callouts
  • Exploded assembly view for build documentation

How quality is validated

  • Interference and motion checks across the reduction stage
  • Zero-backlash mesh verified by stack-up, then on the dyno
  • First production run assembled with zero reworks
  • FEA spot-checks under peak motor torque
