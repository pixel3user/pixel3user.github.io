Video Production

Editing, color grading, and finishing

Post-production run as an engineering pipeline: structured ingest, offline edit, XML conform, colour-managed grading, standards-based audio mastering, and technical QC against explicit delivery specs. Every master — archive, broadcast, and social — is checked against numbers, not eyeballed.

Tools surveyed in this file: OpenShot · Shotcut · Lightworks · Kdenlive · Olive Video Editor

How this page is organised: engineering decision first, visual mechanism second, claim-by-claim audit third, and the retained files that would settle the claim last.

Audio compliance

Loudness is a measured delivery condition

How to read it: Request the Fairlight loudness report or an ffprobe/ebur128 log for each exported file. One master passing does not validate the others.

Mastering chain

Archive, broadcast, and social are separate promises

How to read it: Vertical reframing deserves its own QC pass: faces, product labels, captions, and safe areas can fail even when the 16:9 master is perfect.

Failure modes worth probing

  • A look node placed before exposure/balance makes one camera match while pushing another outside recoverable range.
  • Spectral de-noising removes room tone changes but introduces watery dialogue artefacts on headphones.
  • True peaks pass in the DAW meter but exceed ceiling after H.264/AAC encoding.
  • Vertical reframing crops product labels or captions that were safe in the 16:9 master.

Three questions that reveal depth

  1. Which node is the creative look, and which nodes are strictly technical transforms?
  2. Were loudness measurements taken on rendered files or only inside the timeline?
  3. How was the vertical version reframed: pan-and-scan per shot or one global crop?

Execution sequence

3-minute commercial brand-launch film, finished to broadcast spec

The source brief in delivery order, rewritten as an inspectable process. Each step should leave behind one of the artefacts in the dossier above; a step with no surviving output is where a reviewer should press hardest.

  1. Ingest and build the rough cut

    Organise 6+ hours of multi-camera 4K Sony S-Log3 footage and product b-roll, then build pacing and structure in Kdenlive and Premiere Pro.

  2. Conform into a colour-managed pipeline

    Move the locked timeline via XML into DaVinci Resolve Studio and grade through a node-based ACES pipeline — balancing exposure and unifying skin tones across mixed cameras.

  3. Master the audio to standard

    Clean dialogue in Fairlight with dynamic EQ and spectral de-noising, then mix effects and score to broadcast loudness: −23 LUFS integrated, −1 dBTP true peak.

  4. Deliver every format from one master

    Export an uncompressed ProRes 422 HQ archive plus 16:9 and vertical H.264 versions normalised to −14 LUFS for web and social delivery.

  5. QC before anyone else does

    Run technical quality control on every deliverable — loudness, true peak, colour gamut, captions-safe framing — resulting in zero delivery rejections.

Expected deliverables

  • Locked edit with XML conform package
  • ACES node-tree grade (Resolve project)
  • Broadcast master: −23 LUFS / −1 dBTP
  • Web/social masters: 16:9 + vertical at −14 LUFS
  • ProRes 422 HQ archive master

How quality is validated

  • Loudness and true-peak metered on every export
  • Skin tones matched across mixed-camera log sources
  • Zero QC rejections across all delivery packages
  • Before/after grade checks on scopes, not monitors alone
