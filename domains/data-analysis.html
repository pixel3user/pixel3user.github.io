Office & Data Analysis

Office suites, statistics, and econometrics

Applied statistics with an audit trail: scripted data cleaning, explicit recoding decisions, models reported with uncertainty, and results translated into tables a non-statistical committee can act on. The same .do file that cleans the data also produces the final numbers — nothing is hand-copied.

Tools surveyed in this file: LibreOffice · EViews · Stata

The engineering decision

Which clinical and care-process factors are independently associated with 30-day heart-failure readmission in 40,000+ admissions — and can another analyst reproduce the result?

This question — not the software logo list — is the organising test for the case. Every visual and artefact below either helps answer it or exposes what is still only an assertion in the survey text.

Failure modes worth probing

  • A predictor recorded after discharge leaks outcome information into an apparently strong model.
  • Repeated admissions from the same patient are treated as independent without discussion or clustering.
  • Complete-case analysis silently removes a sicker subgroup and changes the apparent risk factors.
  • Good AUC hides poor calibration, so predicted readmission risks are systematically too high or low.

Three questions that reveal depth

  1. How were repeat admissions and transfers handled in the 30-day outcome definition?
  2. Which variables were prespecified, and which were selected after seeing results?
  3. Was performance assessed by calibration and subgroup, or only by overall AUC?

Execution sequence

30-day heart-failure readmission risk: an EHR cohort study

The source brief in delivery order, rewritten as an inspectable process. Each step should leave behind one of the artefacts in the dossier above; a step with no surviving output is where a reviewer should press hardest.

  1. Assemble the cohort in Stata

    Clean, recode, and merge five years of electronic health records and discharge data — over 40,000 admissions — with every transformation scripted and re-runnable.

  2. Define variables before modelling

    Fix comorbidity flags, prescription-adherence measures, and follow-up timing windows up front, so model choices cannot quietly chase significance.

  3. Fit multivariable logistic regression

    Estimate adjusted odds ratios for clinical and process-of-care factors, reporting 95% confidence intervals alongside every point estimate.

  4. Evaluate discrimination

    Assess model performance with ROC curves and AUC, checking that the model ranks risk usefully rather than merely fitting the sample.

  5. Brief the decision-makers

    Summarise risk factors and clinical takeaways into visual tables and a briefing report in LibreOffice Writer and Calc for the quality-improvement committee.

Expected deliverables

  • Commented Stata .do pipeline (clean → model → tables)
  • Adjusted odds-ratio table with 95% CIs and p-values
  • ROC curve with AUC summary
  • One-page executive briefing (LibreOffice)

How quality is validated

  • 40,000+ admissions after documented exclusions
  • Adjusted (not crude) odds ratios throughout
  • Discrimination quantified by ROC/AUC
  • Tables exported by script — no manual transcription
