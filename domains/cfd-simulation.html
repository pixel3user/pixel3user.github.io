Physics Simulation: CFD

Fluid flow and thermal analysis

Thermal-fluid simulation held to an experimental standard: mesh independence demonstrated, near-wall resolution controlled, design trades quantified, and predictions correlated against physical measurements. A contour plot is only useful if the number behind it survives contact with a thermocouple.

Numerical credibility

Mesh independence is a sequence, not an adjective

How to read it: The STAR-CCM+ case should expose mesh metrics and monitor histories. If only the finest mesh is shown, independence has been asserted, not demonstrated.

System trade-off

A colder plate can still be the worse design

How to read it: Ask for flow rate, coolant inlet temperature/properties, heat load, ΔP, pumping power, and thermocouple locations behind the ±1.5 °C correlation.

Failure modes worth probing

  • Auto-scaled contours make a worse design look cooler by changing the colour limits.
  • A coarse prism layer predicts the wrong wall heat transfer while residuals still converge smoothly.
  • Manifold redistribution reduces average ΔT but starves one module at low flow.
  • Thermocouples measure plate surface while simulation reports cell core, creating false agreement.

Three questions that reveal depth

  1. What heat-generation profile represents fast charging, and is it transient or steady?
  2. Which monitored quantity reached mesh independence first: ΔT, ΔP, or local Nusselt number?
  3. Where were thermocouples placed relative to the simulated temperature probes?

Execution sequence

EV battery-pack cold-plate optimisation by conjugate heat transfer

The source brief in delivery order, rewritten as an inspectable process. Each step should leave behind one of the artefacts in the dossier above; a step with no surviving output is where a reviewer should press hardest.

  1. Set up multi-region CHT

    Model solid cells, cold plate, and coolant as coupled regions in STAR-CCM+ so interface temperatures and heat fluxes are solved, not assumed.

  2. Sweep the channel geometry

    Use design exploration over serpentine and mini-channel layouts, cutting maximum cell temperature delta by 22% across fast-charging profiles.

  3. Earn the right to trust the mesh

    Run mesh-independence studies with low-y+ wall treatment to resolve thermal boundary layers and Nusselt-number distributions at fluid–solid interfaces.

  4. Trade pressure drop against cooling

    Balance coolant pumping power and pressure-drop penalties against heat-transfer gains, so the chosen design wins on system efficiency, not just peak temperature.

  5. Correlate against the rig

    Compare predictions with thermocouple measurements from physical thermal-rig testing — agreement within ±1.5 °C.
